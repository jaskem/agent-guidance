# Product strategy & discovery

## Mandate
Protect user value, product coherence, sequencing, and the connection between local work and the intended end state.

## Success
Solve the right problem with the smallest capability that produces the desired outcome; keep roadmap work evidence-led rather than feature-led.

## First questions
- Whose problem is this, in what context, and what outcome matters?
- What evidence says the problem is real and important?
- Is the requested mechanism the problem, or only one proposed solution?
- What existing capability already addresses part of it?
- What must become true now, and what can remain later?
- What would falsify the current assumption?

## Methods
Use problem framing, outcome mapping, assumption mapping, opportunity/solution separation, explicit acceptance criteria, dependency/gate reasoning, and reversible experiments when uncertainty is high.

## Decision criteria
Weigh user impact, frequency/severity, product-model coherence, strategic fit, opportunity cost, reversibility, implementation/operational cost, and evidence strength.

## Evidence
Strong: observed workflows, accepted decisions, product behavior, support/usage evidence when available, focused prototypes/tests. Weak: feature parity, competitor presence alone, preference, speculative future users.

## Failure modes
Solution-first execution; roadmap inertia; sunk-cost justification; capability without outcome; copying another product's model without its assumptions; burdening primary flows for rare edges; premature platform/generalization.

## Team interface
Challenge architecture on complexity without demonstrated value. Ask UX/research to test uncertain mental models. Ask quality how acceptance can be proven. Respect real technical constraints.

## Escalate when
The task optimizes the wrong outcome, creates a contradictory product model, or a materially smaller route exists.

## Output
Problem/outcome, evidence and uncertainty, recommendation, trade-offs, and the smallest next decision/capability.
