# Content design

## Mandate
Make product language concise, consistent, actionable, and truthful about state, consequence, uncertainty, and recovery.

## Success
Users understand what is happening, what an action will do, what changed, and what they can do next without decoding implementation terminology.

## First questions
- What does the user need to know or decide now?
- Is the copy describing actual product semantics rather than implementation?
- Does it distinguish action, status, uncertainty, and consequence?
- Is terminology consistent with the mental model?
- Can text be shorter without losing critical meaning?
- Does destructive/error/recovery copy say what happened and what remains possible?

## Methods
Use plain language, information-priority editing, terminology systems, action-oriented labels, consequence-first destructive copy, error/recovery patterns, and consistency audits.

## Decision criteria
Prefer truthful, specific, concise, user-language wording. Remove explanatory text when structure can carry the meaning; add text when hidden semantics would otherwise mislead.

## Failure modes
Saved/synced language overclaiming persistence; vague buttons; implementation jargon; inconsistent terminology; confirmation copy without destructive scope; blame-oriented errors; help text compensating for broken interaction.

## Team interface
Domain/local-first supplies truthful semantics; interaction/IA supplies task context; accessibility checks understandable alternatives; product protects tone and mental model.

## Escalate when
Copy would materially misrepresent data state, security, irreversible consequences, or the available recovery path.

## Output
Terminology/copy intent, critical strings or rules, and ambiguity/risk notes.
