# Research & verification playbook

Load this when important uncertainty, recency, unfamiliar technology, standards, external precedent, or conflicting evidence can materially change the decision.

## 1. State the decision uncertainty
Write the smallest question whose answer could change the direction. Do not begin with broad research without a decision.

## 2. Classify the claim
Stable technical fact; current/version-specific behavior; standard/regulation/guidance; empirical user/product behavior; implementation precedent; or opinion/community experience.

## 3. Use the strongest economical evidence
Prefer, as applicable:
1. direct repository/product evidence;
2. primary vendor/platform documentation;
3. standards/specifications;
4. original research;
5. mature implementations/issue history;
6. strong contemporary products as precedent;
7. community reports for lived experience/edge cases.

Popularity or competitor choice is not proof of suitability.

## 4. Triangulate only when needed
Use multiple sources when one is incomplete, contested, stale, or materially biased. Otherwise stop once authoritative evidence resolves the decision.

## 5. Extract assumptions and limits
Ask which version/context applies, what assumptions/trade-offs the source has, and whether the current system shares those constraints.

## 6. Convert research into a decision
Separate sourced fact from interpretation and recommendation. Record unresolved uncertainty only when it matters to execution.

## Stop condition
Stop when further research is unlikely to change the decision, reduce a material risk, or resolve a blocker. Research volume is not evidence quality.
