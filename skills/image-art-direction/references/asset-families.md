# Reusable asset families

Choose an asset class and its purpose before making a style library. For photography define subject relationship, framing, depth, light, material and permitted retouching; for illustration define geometry, mark/edge treatment, palette and detail; for icons define semantics, optical size, stroke/fill and family consistency; for texture define scale, contrast, tiling and where it may appear. Do not force every class into a campaign.

Use an accepted anchor plus a small varied set of subjects to test whether the language generalizes. Hold essential identity cues fixed and vary the requested subject, crop or format deliberately. Inspect assets in their final containers and contexts, including transparent edges, contrasting backgrounds and responsive crops. Each asset should have a distinct communication job. A contact sheet aids comparison but does not replace required individual outputs.

Preserve approved logos as original vector/asset files during application; generated approximations are studies, not faithful logo masters. Keep exact factual diagrams and product geometry with evidence-based drawing tools. Record source/author, accepted variant, intended use, rights/license evidence, meaningful alternative text where applicable and important crop/transparency limits in the existing asset owner. Unknown rights are unresolved, not assumed permission from a moodboard.

The supporting specification should state fixed features, allowed variations and rejection examples; avoid a list of attractive adjectives without inspectable anchors. Reuse accepted directions and revise concrete defects. A material unknown can block one dependent asset without forcing a new approval ceremony for the whole set.
