# Narrative formats and production contracts

Choose the format from the audience, message and delivery context, not the availability of a template or API. Use existing approved content and assets; the following branches refine the same narrative workflow.

| Format | Additional contract and evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Captions, kinetic type and short-form clips | Preserve the actual words and speaker/timing source. Check reading time, line breaks, safe crop and smallest output. Word-level highlights require real alignment; do not fabricate transcripts or timing. |
| Data, diagrams, mathematics or maps | Verify values, units, scale, time and geographic/structural relationships before choreography. Separate source data, semantic model, visual scale and display formatting. A complete static composition should communicate correctly before it moves. Use exact plotting, diagram or mathematical rendering tools for factual content. |
| Template batches and personalized summaries | Keep data separate from scene logic. Test actual props plus long, empty, sparse, zero and negative cases where meaningful. Preserve required content rather than silently dropping it to fit. |
| Product, advertising or testimonial pieces | Ground features, quoted statements and product geometry in real sources. Keep supplied claims and illustrative scenes distinct; use approved logo assets and inspect the final lockup. |
| Talking-head, interviews, voice and B-roll | Identify the authorized source/persona, transcript and source rights. Keep actual speech, stock illustration and generated scenes distinguishable. Use a chosen supported provider only within the request; an available credential is not a provider-selection or spending instruction. |
| Countdown, loop, bumper or lower third | Define the sampled-frame contract, safe area, hold/exit behavior and intended terminal value. Check the actual last encoded frame and loop boundary; a formula that approaches zero may never show zero on the final sample. |

For transparent overlays, inspect actual decoded alpha on light and dark footage, the intended blank/hold/exit frames, longest names and masking boundaries. A codec label alone does not prove transparency in the destination editor/browser. For maps, use one projection/coordinating model for map, markers and routes, including relevant wrap/antimeridian cases. For mathematics, verify terms, axes and glyphs before animation.

When enriching existing speaker footage, preserve the accepted recording/audio and choose per beat whether to show the speaker, an overlay or supporting imagery. Keep factual reveals aligned to the actual speech anchor; an attractive early reveal can undermine the explanation. Model scene/shot actions and camera changes on the same timeline and preserve tracked object/pivot identity across cuts.

For narration and sound, measure actual clip lengths before final timing. Account for transition overlap in the total frame budget. Listen when claiming audio quality or synchronization; energy-onset and loudness checks are supporting measurements with their own coverage limits. Keep fonts, color pipeline, delivery profile and compression requirements tied to the actual target specification rather than a universal recipe.

For asynchronous generation/export, preserve the request identity and known result at the existing work owner. Check whether a timed-out request succeeded before retrying; resume/poll the existing job when supported. Keep cost authority distinct from timeout, duration or frame budgets. Do not probe keys, install a paid runner or silently switch providers because a sample script chooses one.

Validate the validators: a browser-start failure, no registered layout objects, no sync events or skipped audio analysis must remain unperformed coverage, not CLEAN. Sampled boxes do not prove glyph fit or unsampled states; metadata probes do not prove decoded content. Keep generated assets, code, actual captures, final encode and playback observations distinct.
